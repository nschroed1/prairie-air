# Prairie Air: first 30 days of marketing

Prepared September 5, 2026 · Budget ceiling: **$1,000 USD** · Destination: [playprairieair.com](https://playprairieair.com/)

## The strategy

Sell the pleasure of flying low over Iowa, then give players a reason to master a clean spray pass. Start with a small, measurable alpha launch: attract players into account-free practice, help them finish a job, and introduce interested pilots to the shared county.

**Positioning:** Prairie Air is a scenic browser flying game where you take farm contracts, master precise crop-dusting passes, and build a career in a shared Iowa county.

**Campaign line:** Big skies. Precise flying. One shared county.

**Primary action:** Fly your first job.

**Supporting promise:** Play the alpha in your desktop browser. No download; no account needed for practice. An account is required for public county jobs and standings.

This plan assumes the alpha remains free to try for this first month and the owner can spend roughly 5–7 hours per week on capture, community replies and reviewing results. These are planning assumptions, not a decision about future pricing. The goal is learning and repeat play; there is no established revenue model or customer lifetime value yet.

## Who to reach

| Priority | Audience hypothesis | Lead with | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Players who enjoy scenic, approachable flying and short skill challenges | Low passes, visible field coverage, a clean turn | First-job completion and another flight |
| 2 | Farming/simulation players curious about a flying game | Working fields, rural detail, farmer contracts and upgrades | Practice completion, interest in the county |
| 3 | Iowa/Midwest and aviation enthusiasts | Recognizable rural scenery and the joy of a crop-duster flyby | Useful feedback and actual play, not just likes |

Treat the audience fit as a hypothesis. Describe the handling as arcade flying; the alpha does not offer runway takeoff/landing or professional flight/agronomic training. Public players share skies and compete for individual jobs; cooperative contract payouts are not implemented.

## What success would look like

Aim for **100 distinct first-job completers** during the month and **20 of those players returning for another flight within seven days**, allowing the final week's return window to mature after day 30. These are stretch planning goals, not forecasts or industry benchmarks.

Supporting targets: ten useful playtest conversations and enough evidence to identify one promising message/channel combination. Signups, followers and video views help diagnose the funnel but are not the main success measure.

The first-month decision is whether to continue recruiting, fix a specific point of friction, or change the audience/message. Profitable acquisition cannot be established before a revenue model exists.

## Prepare the game before buying traffic

- Finish the two highest-priority polish items: remove flight HUD overlaps and help a beginner recover after missing the first field at zero coverage.
- Observe at least five new players, from opening the game through trying to finish the starter. Record completion time instead of promising a three-minute session before it is demonstrated.
- Check production signup, verification, return-to-game, claim, completion and payout with test pilots. Run a small simultaneous session before promoting public multiplayer.
- Make the campaign destination show gameplay quickly, retain the guest-play CTA and explain the desktop keyboard controls. Keep the game's current visual identity.
- Add and verify the measurement events below. A source search did not find a dedicated marketing funnel tracker in the current app; do not assume it is already collecting these metrics.
- Record the current build after polish. Verify low-flight animals and weather visuals before featuring them prominently in ads. Use actual gameplay and accurately label the alpha.

**Initial paid-test gate:** no unresolved blocker in starting or finishing practice, verified attribution, and at least 10 of the first 20 distinct recruited starters completing the first job. Small-sample results are directional. If this gate fails, use the planned week for fixes and another small playtest; the media budget stays unspent.

## Channel mix

### 1. Gameplay video: discovery and message testing

Create six original clips in the month and adapt the same footage for YouTube Shorts and one secondary short-video account, such as Instagram Reels. Choose the secondary account based on an existing audience or willingness to maintain it; do not create five empty channels.

Each clip should show motion immediately, communicate one idea, and end with “Fly your first job — playprairieair.com — desktop browser.” Use captions and engine/ambient sound so the story also works muted. Suggested formats are 15–25 seconds, with a vertical edit and a landscape master.

YouTube Shorts descriptions and comments do not provide clickable URLs. Use a channel profile link and show the short domain on screen. A longer gameplay video can provide an additional clickable route when the account has the required external-link features. [YouTube's link guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639)

### 2. Player communities and small creators: qualified first flights

Contribute to a small number of relevant conversations and publish two substantive gameplay posts where developer promotion is permitted. Disclose that you are making the game, include a real clip and direct play link, and ask one useful question. Candidate communities include indie-game/playtest spaces and appropriate farming or aviation groups; check each group's current rules before selecting a posting slot. Public rule access was incomplete during this research, so no community is pre-cleared for promotion.

Shortlist ten creators based on recent relevant videos, understandable commentary, genuine player discussion and a willingness to try an alpha. Prioritize fit over follower count. Send up to five personalized invitations in week 2 and five in week 3, after the owner approves the recipient list and messages. One follow-up after 5–7 days is enough. No outreach is sent by this plan.

The creator budget can fund up to two small, clearly disclosed trial collaborations if quotes fit. It is an allocation, not a claim that creators will accept a particular rate. Unpaid editorial interest remains welcome; payment is never conditional on a positive opinion.

### 3. Reddit ads: one contained paid experiment

Start with one US, English-language, desktop-focused gaming audience and two creative messages. Use flight/simulation/indie community or interest options actually available in Ads Manager, without layering so many filters that the audience becomes unusable. Reddit documents community, interest and desktop/mobile targeting; confirm those controls in the chosen campaign flow before enabling spend. This platform is a testable fit, not a proven acquisition winner. [Reddit targeting documentation](https://www.business.reddit.com/learning-hub/articles/how-reddit-ads-targeting-works-for-smbs)

Use the same audience, destination and format for both messages so the first comparison tests the hook. Give each concept an explicit $50 initial allocation, rather than relying on automatic delivery to split $100 equally. This is a directional pilot, not a statistically powered A/B test.

- **A — Scenic escape:** “Your next flying break is over Iowa.” A low pass above crops and a rural landmark, followed by a clear guest-play CTA.
- **B — Precision challenge:** “Can you cover 95% without spraying past the field?” A clean pass, visible coverage and the actual overspray deduction when the aircraft leaves the field.

Use traffic acquisition initially if conversion optimization lacks enough measured events; judge results by first-party completed flights. Put “desktop browser” in the copy. If desktop targeting is unavailable, hold the spend until the landing/game experience has been verified for the devices that would receive it.

**Later distribution:** itch.io could offer another discovery route if a playable practice build is packaged and tested for it. The current Cloudflare application is not assumed to be a drop-in HTML upload. itch.io requires a purchasable, downloadable or browser-playable project for indexing; a page that only links out is not a reliable discovery strategy. Defer that packaging from this month's critical path. [itch.io indexing requirements](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-indexed), [HTML5 build requirements](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5)

## Budget: a ceiling, not a spending obligation

| Allocation | Maximum | Release condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Reddit acquisition test | $400 | $100 pilot after readiness gate; remaining $300 only after review |
| Small creator trials | $200 | Quotes, relevant audience and agreed deliverables fit the cap |
| Clip editing/captions | $150 | Actual footage ready; keep unspent if editing is done in-house |
| Five playtest thank-yous | $100 | $20 each for time and candid feedback; exclude these testers from acquisition results |
| Contingency | $150 | Tracking fixes, creative revisions, or extra distribution after a documented decision |
| **Total** | **$1,000** | No automatic purchases or campaign activation |

These are all-in planning caps; platform fees or taxes must fit within them. Existing hosting and owner development time are outside this incremental marketing budget. Do not borrow from the reserve automatically to disguise a failed test.

**Paid decisions:** after the initial $100, proceed only if attribution works, players can complete, and the cost per first completer is around $8 or lower. That $8 is an experimental spending ceiling, not a market benchmark or profitability target. At least 13 attributable completers from $100 would meet it. If the result is worse, inspect the funnel and revise before releasing the remaining money. Stop immediately for broken flows; do not buy more traffic to diagnose a known bug.

At $8 per first completer, $400 would buy 50 completions arithmetically; actual performance may be much worse. Reaching the 100-person monthly goal therefore requires organic/creator contributions or better paid performance. Report media cost per completion separately from the blended cost of all marketing work.

Keep the weekly operating rhythm small: one capture/edit block, one recruitment block, short replies on posting days, and one results review. If creator replies consume the available time, reduce secondary-platform posting first.

## Six clips and a reusable press kit

| Clip | Opening hook | What must be visible | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Scenic pass | “Your next flying break is over Iowa.” | Aircraft moving over crops, road/river/farm landmark | Fly your first job |
| 2. Precision | “95% coverage. How clean can you fly?” | Real coverage increase, boundary and spray-off turn | Try the starter |
| 3. Rural life | “Take a closer look at the pasture.” | Verified cows, sheep/goats or a farm worker in a low pass | Explore the county scenery |
| 4. Weather | “The wind changes your approach.” | Visible forecast plus actual drift/flight effect | Try the crosswind lesson |
| 5. Improvement | “Same pilot. Cleaner second pass.” | Genuine first/second attempt and debrief comparison | Beat your own first flight |
| 6. Shared county | “One county. Every completed field stays completed.” | Verified two-player flight and current open fields | Join the announced session |

If a shot cannot be captured truthfully, use a proven scenic or skill clip instead. Preserve real HUD values. Avoid promising animals react to spray, equal-gear public competition, cooperative payouts, mobile readiness or a populated world that has not been demonstrated.

The compact press kit should contain: a 60-word description, transparent logo, three current landscape screenshots, a 20-second landscape trailer, a vertical cut, controls, current alpha limitations, direct play URL and an owner-selected contact email. The current screenshot review is useful internally; recapture promotional footage after the HUD changes.

## Multiplayer promotion must match available work

The current alpha has 60 finite jobs per seven-day season, released in three waves of 20. It admits up to 32 connected pilots, but that limit is not a demonstrated load-tested capacity. Practice is separate from the public economy.

Start public play sessions with 6–8 invited pilots only after a successful smaller concurrency check. Check available jobs immediately before invitations and again before the session; aim for at least two open jobs per expected participant. Reduce the invite count or move the session if work is unavailable. Respect the existing season rather than resetting completed fields for marketing.

Broad campaign links lead to practice. Describe public sessions as scheduled chances to meet other pilots, not a guarantee of continuously busy skies. Publish real county progress, with permission for identifiable player quotes/clips. Cosmetic recognition and shareable debrief cards are possible follow-up features, not current campaign promises.

## Measurement and the weekly decision

Instrument these before the paid test. Use a pseudonymous browser/player identifier, session ID, timestamp, build version, practice/public mode and campaign source. Do not put emails or tokens in URLs or event payloads. Same-browser practice identity is approximate; browser resets and cross-device visits limit deduplication.

| Event | Meaning |
|---|---|
| campaign_visit | Landing visit with source/medium/campaign/content captured |
| game_ready | Scene and controls are ready for play |
| practice_started | Player starts the first job, not just opens its briefing |
| first_coverage | First nonzero effective coverage in that attempt |
| practice_completed | First starter payout/debrief, deduplicated per player for acquisition reporting |
| next_flight_started | Another flight starts; keep same-session continuation separate from a return |
| signup_verified | Account verification succeeds |
| public_job_claimed / public_job_completed | Successful server-confirmed public outcomes |
| flight_session_started | A session with actual flying, for return measurement |

Use consistent UTMs, for example `?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=alpha_30d&utm_content=precision_a`. Give each creator and community post its own content value. Attribute a first completion to the stored first campaign touch within a seven-day window; keep direct/unattributed traffic separate. Preserve the source through signup and exclude owner, QA, incentivized and automated test sessions from acquisition totals. Do not add noisy per-frame marketing events.

| Metric | Definition / decision use |
|---|---|
| Ready rate | Unique ready visitors / unique measured landing visitors; loading failures here precede any gameplay conclusion |
| Start rate | Unique first-job starters / unique ready visitors |
| First-job completion | Unique first-job completers / unique starters, grouped by first-start cohort and source |
| Immediate continuation | Completers who begin another flight in the same session / completers |
| Seven-day return | Completers who fly in a new session 24–168 hours later / completers whose full seven-day observation window has elapsed; this is not exact-day-7 retention |
| Media cost per first completer | Media spend / attributable first completers; report numerator and denominator |
| Public completion | Completed claimed jobs / claimed jobs, alongside expirations and errors |

If visits fail to become ready sessions, fix loading/device support. If ready players do not start, revise the opening promise and CTA. If starters cannot finish, fix guidance/handling. If completers never fly again, prioritize a stronger second job and progression. If practice works but public play loses people, fix account/claim availability before adding county promotions.

Review return data only for mature cohorts and show raw counts. Compare sources directionally; a small $1,000 launch cannot prove a universal winning channel. At $0 completions the acquisition cost is undefined, not zero. Keep a simple weekly table of spend, visitors, ready players, starters, first completers, second flights, eligible returners, observed returners and the next action.
